# Fact-check: pre-feasibility of precast RCC construction in Bangladesh

**Research cut-off:** 19 November 2026. “Correct” means the claim is supported as stated or within its stated scope; “wrong” means the numeric or contextual assertion conflicts with its cited source; “unverifiable” means no reliable source located for the exact assertion. Market reports are commercial estimates, not peer-reviewed measurements; report year and scope must accompany them.

## A. Claim-by-claim findings

| Claim | Verdict | Correct statement to use | Source(s) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Global precast concrete market approaches USD 986 billion by 2024 | **Wrong** | No credible source located for USD 986B. A current commercial estimate from Grand View Research values the global precast concrete market at **USD 117.1B in 2025**, forecasting **USD 191.0B by 2033** (2026 edition). Market definitions differ; identify this explicitly as a market-research estimate. | [1] |
| 2. Precast reduces construction time by 30–40% | **Unverifiable as a general rule** | Avoid a universal percentage. Time savings are project- and scope-specific: a documented precast double-wall case reported about **40% reduction in the framing period**; a comparative study of precast slabs in two Lima residential projects found **14% better time performance**. State the case and measured scope. | [2], [3] |
| 3. Prefabricated buildings cost 26.3–72.1% more (Alshamrani 2022) | **Wrong** | Alshamrani does not report that range. The indexed study summary reports total cost **SAR 2.21M** for the conventional system and **SAR 1.76–2.07M** for alternatives, described as **6–25% lower** than conventional. This is a Saudi townhouse comparison within its model assumptions, not a generic prefab premium. The article is *Journal of Asian Architecture and Building Engineering*, 21(5), 2058–2073, DOI below. | [4], [5] |
| 4. Precast is ~25% of Saudi market vs ~3% Egypt (Sameh 2021) | **Unverifiable** | Sameh’s AUC master’s thesis exists and studies Egypt’s adoption barriers/market context, but the repository abstract does not substantiate the paired Saudi 25% / Egypt 3% market-share figures. Do not cite these percentages without page-level evidence and a definition of “market share.” | [6] |
| 5. Tushar thesis: 50% OPC replacement reduces CO₂ 54.7% and primary energy 76% vs brick veneer | **Wrong source type; figures substantially correct in a different source** | The figures are in a **2022 peer-reviewed article**, not a 2024 RMIT PhD thesis. It reports a **100-mm external precast concrete wall with 50% fly ash** (not necessarily “OPC replacement” as a generalized mix prescription) reducing CO₂ by **54.7%** and energy demand by **75.9%** versus a **110-mm brick-veneer benchmark**. The 125-mm panel case gave lower reductions. | [7] |
| 6. India affordable precast housing ~INR 1,200/sq ft (~USD 155/m²), Kankuntla et al. 2020 | **Partly correct, misleading generalization** | The cited paper’s modeled **single G+3 building** gives ₹1,200/ft² for prefabricated versus ₹1,400/ft² conventional (its table; costs are study-specific). It is not a national affordable-housing benchmark. ₹1,200/ft² converts to about 12,917 INR/m²; any USD conversion must state exchange-rate date and is not supported as a timeless USD 155/m². | [8] |
| 7. Malaysia IBS mandated for government projects; 70% score | **Correct with policy chronology caveat** | CIDB’s official guideline traces the IBS Roadmap **2003–2010**, the 2005 government-project requirement for at least **50% IBS content**, and Treasury Circular Letter dated **31 October 2008**, directing **70 IBS Score** for applicable federal public projects. The subsequent roadmap covered **2011–2015**. A later CIDB page describes 70-score mandatory rules for specified projects; say which date/instrument and project threshold is meant rather than implying all government projects at all times. | [9], [10] |
| 8. India PMAY/GHTC/LHP and IS 11447 / IS 15916 | **Correct with scope clarification** | PMAY-U launched **25 June 2015**. MoHUA launched the Lighthouse Projects challenge under GHTC-India in **February 2019**; six LHP sites received foundation stones **1 January 2021**. These initiatives include alternative construction technologies, but do not imply every PMAY home used precast. BIS titles/years: **IS 11447:1985, Code of practice for construction with large panel prefabricates**; **IS 15916:2010, Building design and erection using prefabricated concrete—Code of practice**. | [11]–[14] |
| 9. BNBC 2020, precast section, integrity ties, ACI, Dhaka seismic zone | **Partly correct; tie-value statement needs correction/qualification** | BNBC 2020 was notified S.R.O. 55-Law/2020 (dated 18 Feb 2020) and published in the Gazette **11 Feb 2021**. **Part 6, Chapter 6, Section 6.10** is “Precast Concrete.” Dhaka is **Seismic Zone 2, Z = 0.15** under BNBC 2020 Table 6.2.22 (the often quoted 0.20 is from another edition/table or an erroneous secondary summary). ACI 318-19 §16.2.5 applies to precast bearing-wall structures ≥3 storeys: longitudinal/transverse ties are **1,500 lb/ft ≈ 21.9 kN/m**; vertical ties **3,000 lb per horizontal foot of wall ≈ 43.8 kN per horizontal metre**. Thus 22 and 44 are rounded equivalents. ACI §16.2.1.3 expressly prohibits connections relying solely on friction caused by gravity loads. Check BNBC’s exact tie clauses directly in the gazette text before claiming they reproduce a particular ACI edition word-for-word. | [15]–[17] |
| 10. Bangladesh literature and institutional/logistical context | **Mixed; some verified, several topics need stronger/targeted sources** | Chowdhury (2022) is a conference paper indexed as ICRICE; accessible abstract reports a questionnaire/RII study of 16 prospects and 15 obstacles. A 2025 peer-reviewed Bangladesh qualitative study interviewed 15 professionals and identifies skills/training, transport infrastructure, high initial cost, limited local manufacturing and coordination as barriers. A peer-reviewed Bangladesh workforce study reports low proportions with full knowledge/practice of safety/materials/equipment/labour/building-code rules and 6.45% professional training (sample/location-specific). BBS census 2022 reports 31.66% urban population; ADB (2026) states urban population grew from 30M (2000) to 71.56M (2024) and housing deficit about 6M, projected 10.5M by 2030. McKinsey’s industry report estimates precast plant break-even at 5,000–8,000 housing units/year (model estimate, not peer-reviewed). A 2019 study cited by a peer-reviewed concrete LCA paper suggests a typical precast-product market radius near 50 km owing to transport cost/low margins; treat as context-specific. RAJUK’s official rules page lists Dhaka Metropolitan Building Construction Regulations 2025; 2008 rules were gazetted 29 May 2008. As of this research date, 2025 rules are listed as operative; avoid describing the revision as merely proposed. | [18]–[25] |
| 11. Bangladesh HGV axle/gross limits (e.g., 10 t single axle) | **Wrong / oversimplified** | Do not state a blanket 10-t single-axle or one gross limit without axle configuration and rule date. RHD’s 2024 draft supervisory manual reproduces maximum vehicle-weight categories in effect since 16 July 2021: e.g., 6-wheel 2-axle categories 13/16/22 t depending on laden class, 3-axle 10-wheel 30 t, 4-axle 14-wheel 40 t, 5-axle 18-wheel 47 t. The 27 Dec 2022 Gazette order sets axle-group/vehicle limits and specifies maximum laden/train weight **48 t for articulated** and **32 t for other vehicles**, with additional constraints (including intermediate axle 8.25 t and axle spacing). Quote the applicable schedule, not a generic single-axle value. | [26], [27] |

## C. Verification notes and limits

- **Claim 3:** The original article’s DOI and bibliographic record were verified, but the precise comparative cost figures were available here via an indexed literature review summary rather than direct access to the article’s results tables. Treat “6–25% lower” as provisional until checked against the publisher PDF.
- **Claim 4:** The AUC thesis is verified; the two-country market-share statistics are not verified in its repository record. The exact thesis pages were not inspected.
- **Claim 7:** The sources are CIDB institutional guidance summarizing the Treasury circular and roadmaps. For legal-grade citation, obtain the original Treasury Circular letter dated 31 October 2008 and quote its scope/threshold directly.
- **Claim 8:** BIS official online listing was located for IS 15916 and the IS 11447 title/year; the standard documents themselves may require purchase. Do not infer adoption rate from policy participation.
- **Claim 9:** The Gazette directly verifies BNBC title/date and Section 6.10 exists. The retrieved Part 6 Section 6.10 extract confirms structural precast coverage. The Dhaka Z=0.15 result follows the BNBC zone map plus Table 6.2.22; cite the map/table in the final manuscript. ACI tie numbers are from ACI 318-19, while BNBC may preserve different clause numbering/rounding. “No friction-only connections” is explicitly ACI §16.2.1.3.
- **Claim 10:** Chowdhury’s bibliographic record does not expose full proceedings metadata, DOI, or page range. Do not treat Academia.edu as a final bibliographic source; locate the actual ICRICE proceedings before journal submission. The 2025 Arafat DOI is omitted on the journal page as retrieved, so source details need checking against Crossref/publisher metadata. The transport market-radius and break-even figures are geography- and model-specific, not universal engineering constants. The workforce percentages are from a bounded survey, not a national census.
- **Claim 11:** The September 2024 RHD manual is explicitly a **final draft**. The 2022 Gazette copy is hosted at a mirror rather than the Bangladesh government Gazette portal in the retrieved results; replace with an official gazette URL if available before publication. The 2021 limits summarized in the draft manual and 2022 schedule should be reconciled by vehicle category and effective date.
