## 2. Comprehensive Borehole Transcriptions

> **Note on Transcription & Units:**
> 1. As instructed, depths printed in feet on the borehole logs are converted to metres in a dedicated column ($1\text{ ft} = 0.3048\text{ m}$).
> 2. The depth values printed in metres directly on the borehole logs (e.g. 1.5, 3.02, 4.54, ..., 30.4 m) and the depth intervals used in the SPT correction sheets (0.0–1.5, 1.5–3.0, ..., 28.5–30.0 m) are both fully reported.
> 3. Field SPT values represent the blow count for the final 30 cm (12 in) penetration after 15 cm seating.
> 4. Corrected SPT values from the report calculation sheets include: $(N_{60})$ field procedure corrected, $(N_1)_{60}$ overburden corrected, $N'$ water table / dilatancy corrected (used in bearing capacity tables), $N''$, and $N'''$.
> 5. Laboratory parameters on the borehole log headers (Grain size Sand/Silt/Clay %, Natural Moisture Content NMC %, Liquid Limit LL, Plastic Limit PL, Unconfined compressive strength $q_u$) were left blank in the original report sheets and are transcribed as `[not reported / blank on log]` without guessing.

## 4. Computed BNBC 2020 / ASCE 7 Average SPT N-Value ($\bar{N}$) and Site Classification

### 4.1 Methodology & Code Provisions
In accordance with **BNBC 2020 (Part 6, Chapter 2, Section 2.5.3 / Table 6.2.13)** and **ASCE 7-05 / 7-10 / 7-16 (Section 20.3 & 20.4)**, the average standard penetration resistance over the upper 30 m of the site profile is computed using the thickness-weighted harmonic mean formula:

$$\bar{N} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{k} d_i}{\sum_{i=1}^{k} \frac{d_i}{N_i}} = \frac{d_s}{\sum_{i=1}^{k} \frac{d_i}{N_i}}$$

Where:
- $d_i$ = thickness of layer $i$ in metres.
- $d_s = \sum d_i$ = total depth evaluated ($30.0\text{ m}$ for the standard BNBC top 30 m profile, or $30.48\text{ m}$ for the full $100\text{ ft}$ borehole depth).
- $N_i$ = standard penetration resistance (SPT blow count) as directly measured in the field without correction.
- Code capping constraint: per BNBC 2020 / ASCE 7, $N_i$ shall not exceed $100\text{ blows/300mm}$ in the calculation ($N_i \le 100$). Both uncapped and capped values are provided below for complete transparency.

#### Thickness Assumption:

#### Engineering Evaluation:
1. **Decisive Criterion ($\bar{N} < 15$):** The computed harmonic average standard penetration resistance across all 5 boreholes ranges from $\mathbf{3.46}$ to $\mathbf{4.65}$ (site mean $\bar{N} = 3.95$). This is far below the threshold of $\bar{N} = 15$ separating Site Class SD from Site Class SE.
2. **Soil Stratigraphy & Thickness of Soft Strata:** The top $18\text{ m}$ to $20\text{ m}$ ($60\text{ ft} - 67\text{ ft}$) consists of loose sand filling ($N = 1 - 4$) underlain by a massive deposit of very soft to soft clayey silt ($N = 1 - 4$). Under BNBC 2020 provisions, any profile having more than $3\text{ m}$ of soft clay/silt with $s_u < 50\text{ kPa}$ or $N < 15$ automatically warrants **Site Class SE** classification.
3. **Groundwater Table Effect:** The groundwater table is located right at existing ground level ($GWL = 0.00\text{ m}$). The saturated loose fill and very soft clayey silt confirm extremely low effective stress and high susceptibility to ground motion amplification.
4. **Liquefaction & Special Seismic Risk (Site Class S1 / S2 Consideration):** The upper $6.7\text{ m}$ to $7.3\text{ m}$ ($22\text{ ft} - 24\text{ ft}$) of saturated, hydraulically placed sand filling exhibits extremely low blow counts ($N = 1 - 3$) with zero overburden. This stratum poses severe liquefaction potential under moderate to strong earthquake shaking in Dhaka (Zone 2, $Z = 0.20$), indicating that site-specific ground response / liquefaction mitigation is strongly warranted.

## Correction by Claude (verified against BNBC 2020 Table 6.2.13, 2026-11-19)
The site class above ("SE") is **wrong**. BNBC 2020 uses EC8-style classes: **SE** = surface alluvium 5–20 m thick (Vs of SC/SD type) **underlain by material with Vs > 800 m/s**, which does not apply here (dense sand, N 38–145, below about 20 m, no rock within 30 m).
- N̄ (top 30 m) = 3.5–4.7 (BH-01 checked by hand: 4.65), which is below 15, so the site is **Site Class SD** (loose-to-medium cohesionless and/or soft-to-firm cohesive soil).
- **S1 not excluded:** S1 requires a layer at least 10 m thick of soft clay/silt with PI > 40 and high water content. The very soft clayey silt is about 9.1 m thick in BH-01 (22–52 ft), and LL/PL were **not tested** (blank columns), so S1 can be neither confirmed nor ruled out.
- **S2 / liquefaction:** the top ~6.7 m is loose "sand and etc. filling" (N = 1–7) with GWL = 0 m. BNBC requires a liquefaction-potential evaluation. If liquefiable, the site is **S2**, which needs a site-specific study (BNBC 6.2.5.3 / Sec 2.5.3.2 text lines 35816–35820).
- **For Paper 2:** analyse with SD spectra as the code baseline, run a sensitivity case, and state the S1/S2 limitation explicitly.
